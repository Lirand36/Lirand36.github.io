Revenue Team Hub

One internal workspace for Sales, Support and Customer Success at a hospitality procurement platform. It sits on top of the tools the teams already use, and greets every role with its own ranked list of next best actions.

The business problem

This project is a vision of how GTM teams should work today: fast, efficient, and in a single tool that works as one revenue machine, instead of a dozen tabs.

Most revenue teams run Sales, Support and Customer Success across seven or more tools: a CRM, a support inbox, an issue tracker, team chat, a data warehouse, an AI model and calendars. Every handoff between teams (a deal closes, a customer escalates, usage drops before a renewal) means switching tools and copying context by hand, and things fall through.

To make the vision concrete, I set it inside a real-world business: a software company selling procurement and AI invoice processing to hotel groups.

How I read the requirements

The key decision was not to replace any tool. The teams keep their CRM, inbox, tracker and chat; the hub works through them. Reps get one UI built around the company’s own world (hotel groups, properties, purchase orders, invoices) instead of generic CRM objects.

From there I designed per role, not per tool:

Role Lands on Core job
Account executive Pipeline, own deals Move deals forward, follow up before prospects go quiet
Sales manager Team pipeline, approvals Approve discounts, coach on stuck deals
Support Inbox Answer within SLA, escalate to engineering cleanly
Customer Success Portfolio, riskiest first Catch churn risk before renewal, run onboarding

Key decisions and trade-offs

1. “Next best action” as the core unit. Everyone lands on a role-specific dashboard: a short summary, four KPIs, and a ranked list of actions, each with one button. Many finish in one click: approve a discount, escalate a conversation, mark an onboarding step done. Each action says what to do and why (“Follow up with Hannah · No reply in 16 days”).

2. One business event, every system updated. Marking a deal closed-won in one click updates the CRM, announces the win in chat, creates an onboarding channel with the checklist, opens an onboarding epic in the tracker, and logs the event to the warehouse.

3. Stage gates that ask only for what the stage needs. Moving a deal forward asks for that stage’s facts (pain and decision maker at Qualified; date, solutions engineer and attendees at Demo; signer and legal contact at Contract), prefilled from the CRM and written back to it. Pipeline data gets better as a side effect of doing the work.

4. An explainable health score. Customer health (0–100) is a weighted sum the CSM can read: usage trend 30%, adoption 15%, support load 20%, platform stability 15%, sentiment 20%. Each part shows its score and its reason, and a renewal within 90 days amplifies other warning signs.

5. Business rules live in one config. Discount approval above 15%, “quiet” after 7 days without a reply, “stuck” after 14 days in a stage, SLAs of 1 hour for Enterprise and 4 hours for everyone else.

6. AI assists, people decide. In the support inbox, one click gives a summary, the customer’s mood, the likely category, a next step and a draft reply, as structured output. The agent chooses whether to use the draft.

How I handled risk

Risk What I built
People seeing or changing what they shouldn’t Role-based access enforced on the server: every API call is checked against one rules file, and AEs can only change their own deals. The UI mirrors it; it doesn’t enforce it.
Fake inbound webhooks Inbound chat and support webhooks are signature-verified
Secrets leaking into logs All outbound calls go through one gateway that logs, times, and redacts secrets
Breaking real systems during a demo Every connector runs in mock mode by default: requests are built exactly as the real API expects, shown in the activity log, and answered with a realistic fake. Each system goes live on its own when credentials are added.
Irreversible actions by mistake Marking a deal lost and resetting data ask for confirmation; escalation to engineering is a separate red button that asks first
Silent failures nobody can trace An activity log tells the story of every action in plain words, with the exact request and response one click down
Escalations missed SLA countdowns, with a single chat alert on breach; Enterprise and upset customers auto-flagged

Architecture

Browser (no build step)
  ⇅ REST + server-sent events (live updates)
Server
  ├─ home / deals      per-role dashboards and sales signals
  ├─ services          business actions ("what happens when a deal closes")
  ├─ access            what each role can see and do
  ├─ activity          plain-language log of every action
  └─ connectors        CRM · inbox · tracker · chat · warehouse · AI · calendar
        └─ one gateway: logging, timing, secret redaction, mock/live switch

Outcome

A working hub with a 6-minute demo script covering every role, deployable as a shareable link, with each integration switchable from mock to live independently.

From prototype to production

  • Back the in-memory demo data with Postgres, kept current by webhooks and scheduled warehouse pulls.
  • Single sign-on with roles from identity-provider groups, records scoped by CRM owner.
  • A job queue with retries and idempotency keys for outbound calls.
  • Credentials in a secret manager instead of environment files.
